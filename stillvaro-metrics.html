This guide includes the latest Stillvaro 2.1 features and previews. Some screens and controls may differ from your installed version.

Open this guide in Your memory → What the numbers mean or Settings → Help → What the numbers mean. Numbers describe saved practice, not intelligence or a guarantee of future recall.

Digit 1 is π’s leading 3. A group has four digits. Recorded-practice comparisons and sections used by earlier anchor tests hold up to five groups (20 digits); practice passages connect up to four groups (16 digits), including overlapping joins and shorter repairs.

Your overview

Record What counts
Digits practised Completed groups continuously from π. Current acquisition requires three clean recalls; later groups also need two successful incoming joins. Watching or opening the next group is not completion.
Best test · digits Whole groups before the first mistake in saved manual tests or qualifying older scheduled full cold checks from π. Saved anchor tests do not increase this record. This is a historical achievement, not a measurement of today’s recall.
Groups to revisit Unique four-digit groups whose join needs practice or which belong to a due passage. A group counts once even when several due passages include it. This counts groups, not review passages or forgotten digits.

The opening 3141 advances after one guided entry; later practice supplies its recall evidence. Completed prefix credit and saved achievements do not fade on a timer. A group can keep its completed practice credit while its join still needs another pass.

Origin insights follow the contiguous practiced area and its next available group. Browsing ahead and practice from another start do not fill gaps in that path.

The overview ring summarizes group categories in the current map evidence. Its totals count groups, not digits or a predicted retention percentage.

Read the sequence map

Each tile represents four digit positions. One tap opens Group details for its exact status, recorded attempts, wrong-tap positions, and review dates. The overview displays a bounded part of the origin sequence. View all lists every origin group directly: search a digit position to find its group.

Map appearance Meaning
Deeper green · Recalled across days Connected recall on at least three distinct local dates in the current evidence set. These are recorded practice dates, not a guarantee of retention or three independent delayed tests.
Pale green · Recalled in sequence Connected recall has been recorded for the join.
Copper · Revisit A join needs practice or a passage including it is due. Group details distinguish the two reasons. Repair and due work take priority over earlier success; due does not mean forgotten.
Neutral · Still learning No connected recall is recorded for the join. Missing evidence is not a failed attempt.

Group details count eligible recorded attempts and how many were recalled without help. Repeated practice contributes more observations, including warm repetitions; the counts do not estimate future recall. Wrong taps have their own count and do not create completed recall attempts. Errors without a recorded digit position stay separate. A review date belongs to an active passage including the group, rather than necessarily to that four-digit group alone.

Recorded practice

Group details includes the summary cards and heatmaps below directly. They are fixed to the group’s containing section of up to twenty digits, with that section’s digit range labeled. The individual attempt, slip, and review records below the cards describe the opened four-digit group; the cards provide section context. There is no section picker or extra Recorded practice link inside Group details.

The separate Recorded practice row in Your memory opens the same cards with a section picker. It initially selects the section with the latest counted attempt when one is available. You can choose another section there. All cards follow the displayed section.

Card Presentation Evidence
Latest saved practice Unaided recall and Help shown each show a latest saved percentage, a neutral change in percentage points to one decimal place, and Earlier/Latest values with actual saved dates, times, and years. First/latest comparison within up to 32 changed saved observations. Unaided recall identifies its retained attempt count; Help has no attempt count. A single snapshot is a first recorded value; an empty history has no value or comparison.
Group by group The primary visual: one row per four digits, with current Unaided and Help percentages. Colors strengthen as percentages increase. Tap a group to inspect it in place. The selected group’s actual unaided successes and retained eligible attempt count are shown together. Help is a separate smoothed value. --- means no measured value, not failure.
Where slips happen Rows identify groups; columns are their 1st–4th positions. Numbers count wrong taps; stronger color means more within the displayed section. Retained practice errors with a valid digit position and attempted digit. Hints are excluded. Unpositioned errors are counted below the map.

The summaries show saved observations; the group heatmap shows current data, so their values can differ. Repeated identical section snapshots appear once at the first saved date of that unchanged stretch. A changed attempt count or outcome remains a distinct observation even if its percentage stays the same. Filtering occurs before the 32-observation limit, so the first comparison value need not be the earliest value ever saved. Empty or older snapshots without retained attempt counts are omitted. Identical rolling aggregates cannot prove that a new attempt occurred.

Changes are latest minus first in percentage points, rounded to one decimal place. A change that rounds to zero reads Unchanged to 0.1 percentage points. They do not classify progress as good or bad, imply daily measurement, or magnify small differences with a plot. With one observation there is no change to calculate; with none there is no measured value.

The current group heatmap initially selects the opened group in Group details, or the first row in standalone Recorded practice. Tapping a row changes the exact current unaided success/retained attempt detail in place. It does not change the opened group’s individual records below the cards. Heatmaps print values, and VoiceOver reads each cell’s group or digit.

What the percentages use

Measure Calculation Interpretation
Unaided Successful unaided outcomes ÷ retained eligible outcomes from ordinary practice, isolated probes, and older practice checks. Guided completions remain in the denominator. Possible answer-search attempts and older outcomes without recall evidence are excluded. Repeating a group adds attempts. The displayed success and attempt counts are retained records, not lifetime totals. Manual tests stay separate.
Help For an eligible successful attempt: 80% previous estimate + 20% answer information supplied. No answers supplied = 0%; all four supplied = 100%. New groups start at 100% while unknown. Section snapshots average groups equally, including new groups; unmeasured heatmap rows show a dash.

Unaided recall and help are not complementary percentages. Help is an independent exponentially weighted average of information supplied, not an error rate or a fraction with an attempt-count denominator. No attempt count is displayed for Help. Successful isolated checks contribute zero help; possible answer-search attempts do not update the estimate.

Slip counts describe events, not failed groups, unique digits, or lifetime totals. One attempt can add several wrong taps. Manual Memory Test misses appear in test results instead. Undo does not remove previously saved mistakes or help. Trying three distinct wrong digits at one position can flag possible answer search; a typo or slow answer alone does not establish it. Older flags may retain earlier rules.

Memory test scores

Result Meaning
Uninterrupted recall Complete groups of four before the first wrong group. A correct retry cannot restart the score. An unfinished group adds nothing.
Digits checked Submitted groups counted once, including misses. Retries add no extra digits.
Personal best Best saved manual test from the same starting group. Twelve digits from digit 21 means twelve from that anchor, not thirty-two from π.
Worth another look Groups missed, even if a later retry succeeds. The range identifies the group, not an exact wrong digit.

Tests check the whole group on its fourth digit. They use Memory only, four attempts per group, no skipping, and no timer. A correct answer advances; four misses or Finish end and save the test. Undo before submission uses no attempt and refunds none. Tests preserve lessons, input, and review schedules.

Expand Test history in Your memory for recent self-tests. All test results opens Memory checks, with the full saved list and up to 24 recent self-tests from π shown as separate points when at least two are available. The gaps between tests are not measurements. Saved anchor tests and older scheduled checks appear separately. Earlier results may retain different scoring precision or skipped groups.

Explorer recall evidence

Explorer shows evidence for each four-digit shape and for its connections. These categories describe recorded outcomes. They do not measure a probability of future recall.

Status What it means
Not yet observed / Not yet connected There is not enough recorded recall evidence for this shape or connection. Separate shape successes do not establish connected recall.
Recalled without help / Recalled together Unaided recall is recorded for this shape or connection.
Recalled across days Unaided recall is recorded on at least three distinct local dates, with no later recorded failure taking priority.
Needs practice The latest recorded outcome needed help or included a mistake. A newer wrong tap can also prompt this status.

Shapes and connections have separate evidence. A successful containing passage can demonstrate a join; a failed longer passage does not identify a failure at every join. Consecutive groups successfully recalled in the same test can establish a connection. Whole-passage evidence remains separate from individual connection ratings.

Tap a shape or connection for its retained attempts, unaided recalls, dates, slips, and review information. The separate clock indicates that a containing passage is due. A due date does not overwrite recorded recall status. Browsing, scrubbing, and playback change no learning evidence and earn no milestones.

Memory milestones

A milestone records a new uninterrupted manual memory-test best from the beginning of π, at every sixteen digits: 16, 32, 48, 64, and so on. Tests from another starting place, scheduled checks, and Explorer playback do not earn these badges. Repeating the same score awards nothing new.

The milestone board keeps each badge and its earned date. Qualifying earlier manual tests populate existing achievements. Later mistakes do not remove a badge. An earned achievement and the current recall evidence around its Explorer circle describe different things: reaching a past test best and the latest recorded practice.

Turning Milestone badges off hides presentation while retaining test results and achievements. Local data erasure removes those records with the rest of your progress.

Your sequence and reviews

Category Evidence
Join needs practice A repair or frozen state takes priority over past success.
Ready to revisit A passage review containing the group is due.
Still learning No connected recall for the incoming join; the opening group is recorded from π.
Recalled across days Connected recall on at least three distinct local dates in the current evidence set.
Recalled in sequence Connected recall with fewer than three qualifying dates.

The first group of a later passage is an entry cue; alone it cannot establish its incoming join. Misses or help can reset the date set. Category colors are separate from the orb’s digit colors and are not retention percentages.

Reviews follow exact passages. Successful recall can schedule one day, then six, then longer intervals. A first unsuccessful recall, including help, can bring that passage back sooner and begins a one-day repair window. Further unsuccessful attempts within that window remain observations in the history without another change to the schedule’s ease or due date. The first successful recovery restores a one-day interval; further immediate success does not extend it. A subsequent due review can advance normally.

The Continue/Pause choice appears after repeated completed unsuccessful recall attempts in a connected passage or repair. It preserves acquired progress, weak connections, and the same empty retry. Choosing Continue or Resume resets the retry budget and trial clock, not learning evidence. A clean recall of the complete original connected passage resets the retry budget; weak connections may still need another recall. Individual wrong taps and hints inside one attempt do not each consume the retry budget.

The numerical learning requirements and review interval rules are unchanged: this update limits repeated scheduling updates during one repair window. New review observations include their context and whether a different overlapping passage was recalled in the preceding day. That flag describes recent practice; it does not establish cold recall or change an overlapping passage’s due-review behavior. A longer successful passage can still replace shorter reviews inside it; overlaps may remain separate. Dates suggest when to return, not how much you will remember.

History and compatibility

Most diagnostic and metric lists retain up to 256 recent entries; recent answer-search flags retain ten per group. Test records are separate. Rounded percentages can change as old observations leave these lists. Previously saved memory cues and anchor-test results remain in local data, although Your memory no longer offers cue editing, cue search, or an anchor-test action. Erase all progress and settings clears local records; device backups are managed separately.

Older summaries may show Studied, Strong now, Still forming, and Ready to revisit as counts of four-digit groups. Their bar is a distribution, not a timeline; an older Digits recalled total can include groups recalled separately. Recalled together records the longest exact passage from π recalled without help in connected practice; separate successes are not added together. That record remains in saved evidence but is not one of the new overview’s three numbers.

Older diagnostic fields remain in saved data: recovery compares successful resumptions with opportunities after a miss; anchor-entry rate uses first-group outcomes from earlier scheduled cold checks, excluding manual anchor tests. These are not displayed in the simplified overview. Error classifications and recent search flags also remain recorded.
