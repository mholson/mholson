This guide includes the latest Stillvaro 2.1 features and previews. Some screens and controls may differ from your installed version.

The art of chunking π. Four digits at a time. Start with 3141, including π’s leading 3. Leave out the decimal point. Your place saves automatically; there is no countdown.

Your practice rhythm

Step What to do
1 · Guide Follow the highlighted digits and watch the shape form.
2 · Memory Recall the group yourself. Use Guide when available for another look.
3 · Connect Join groups into longer passages. A rewind brings earlier digits back for practice.

The opening 3141 advances after one guided entry. Later practice checks it without help. New groups need repeated unaided recall and connections to earlier groups; one successful answer does not always introduce new digits. Misses and help bring difficult joins back more often. A slow answer alone is not treated as guessing.

Pause a difficult connection

After repeated unsuccessful recall attempts while connecting or repairing a passage, A moment to reset offers Continue practice or Pause for now. The choice appears after a completed attempt, so a wrong tap or hint does not interrupt unfinished input.

Pausing keeps the same place, saved progress, and connections that still need work. It does not skip a group or count the passage as learned. When you return, Practice paused offers Resume practice. Continue or Resume starts the next attempt at the saved place; a fully unaided recall of the original passage is still needed to complete the connection.

A first unsuccessful recall can bring that passage’s review sooner. Further mistakes within its one-day repair window stay in history without repeatedly reducing the longer-term review spacing. A first successful recovery keeps the interval at one day; immediate repeats do not advance it to a longer interval. The usual four-digit groups and requirements for learning and connecting them are unchanged.

Enter and correct

Action Gesture
Enter a digit Tap, or drag onto it and pause until the small ring fills.
Correct a practice slip Let the wrong digit fade, then try that position again. Accepted digits stay in place.
Undo the latest digit Hold it in the four-digit entry row before the fourth digit submits. VoiceOver offers Undo last digit.
Continue Begin when the next empty entry and dial are ready, even while the previous shape settles into the trail.

Undo removes only the latest digit and its stroke. It preserves earlier mistakes, help, and spent test attempts. Completed groups and Watch cannot be edited. Some trail answers stay hidden during recall.

Read Your memory

Your memory starts with three numbers: Digits practised, Best test · digits, and Groups to revisit. Practised digits describe completed progress from π; Best test is your saved uninterrupted test record. Groups to revisit counts each four-digit group once if its join needs practice or a passage containing it is due. It does not count forgotten digits.

On an expanded display, the overview places its summary beside the sequence map; its ring summarizes groups in the displayed categories. The sequence map gives each group of four a tile. Deeper green means connected recall on at least three local dates; pale green means connected recall has been recorded. Copper means a join needs practice or a review is due. Neutral means no connected recall is recorded yet. These colors describe evidence and review work, not certainty about memory.

Tap a tile to open Group details directly. Saved-value summaries and current heatmaps for its containing section appear on the same screen, followed by the group’s individual attempt, slip, and review records. View all lists four-digit groups in your origin sequence. Search a digit position to find its group. Practice from another starting point stays separate.

Your memory no longer offers cue editing, cue search, or an anchor-test action. Previously saved cues and anchor-test results remain in local data.

Expand Test history for recent results and All test results for the full history. A saved best does not predict what you will recall today.

Saved values and current heatmaps

In Group details, the cards below are fixed to the group’s containing section of up to twenty digits. The labeled digit range identifies their scope. They appear directly, with no section picker or extra Recorded practice link.

To compare another section, open Your memory → Recorded practice. This separate screen keeps its section picker and starts with the section containing the latest counted attempt when available.

Card Read it this way
Latest saved practice Unaided recall and Help shown each show their latest saved value and an Earlier/Latest comparison with dates and times. Changes use percentage points to one decimal place, without grading the direction as good or bad. A single observation is a first recorded value; no saved observations means no comparison.
Group by group The main visual shows current unaided and help percentages for each four-digit group. Tap a group to inspect its actual unaided successes out of retained eligible attempts without leaving the screen. Stronger color means higher; --- means no measured value.
Where slips happen Columns are the 1st–4th positions in each group. Counts and stronger color show where more wrong taps were saved.

The summaries compare the first and latest observations within a window of up to 32 changed saved observations, using their actual saved dates, times, and years. Repeated unchanged snapshots appear once at their first date; equal percentages with new attempt counts remain separate. Changes too small to show at one decimal place read Unchanged to 0.1 percentage points. The values and changes are shown as numbers, without a plot. A saved summary can differ from the current heatmap because practice may have changed since that snapshot.

In Group details, the opened group starts selected in the heatmap; the separate Recorded practice screen starts with the first row. Tapping another row changes the current success/attempt detail in place. The individual records below the cards still describe the group you opened.

Unaided is successes divided by retained eligible outcomes, including guided completions in the denominator. Help is an independent smoothed measure of answer information supplied; it is neither the complement of Unaided nor an error rate, and it has no attempt count. These are records of practice, not forecasts of future recall.

For definitions, open Your memory → What the numbers mean or Settings → Help → What the numbers mean. Recorded practice and What the numbers mean are visible rows in Your memory. See the metric reference.

Explore at your pace

Control What it offers
Watch Start from your current group or π; choose 1–8 repeats and Slow, Flow, or Quick speed. Stop to resume your saved input. Watching gives no unaided recall credit.
Practice toolbar → Explore π Swipe the shapes or digit cards, scrub, or use previous/next to move through one continuous chain. Play draws each shape and connecting join; the underline follows the active digit. Change the pace while it plays.
Practise from here Start practice at the selected shape. Tap a connection for its evidence and focused practice. The named return control restores your previous region and unfinished input.
Practice toolbar → Spiral Play the Memory Spiral, or pause to pan and pinch to zoom. Fit the whole spiral or focus the current movement.

Explorer includes all the bundled digits of π. The continuous journey replaces the separate 4/8/16 browsing scales; practice still builds four-digit groups into passages of eight and sixteen. Shapes and digit cards move together, and the drawing continues behind floating controls while the selected shape stays clear.

Glyph strokes and digits keep their Spiral colours. Translucent circles show recorded recall; join symbols describe connected recall. Green checks mark unaided success, and copper revisit arrows identify places needing practice. A gap can mark either an unobserved join or one needing practice; tap its symbol for the exact evidence. A separate clock marks a due review without erasing earlier success. Recalling groups separately does not establish that you recalled their connection.

Browsing, scrubbing, and playback do not award recall or milestones. Tap a shape or connection to see its evidence. The metric guide explains the categories.

Your Watch speed also sets how quickly completed shapes travel from the dial into the sequence during Guide, Memory practice, and memory tests. The next entry opens after the shape lands. Viewing answers during practice counts as guidance. After a practice mistake, finish correcting the group before requesting another demonstration.

Memory milestones

Milestones are earned at 16, 32, 48, 64 digits, and every additional sixteen, for a new uninterrupted personal best from the beginning of π in a manual memory test. Repeating a result does not award the same badge again. Earlier qualifying tests supply existing milestones, and later mistakes do not remove them.

Open the medal button beside Spiral, or tap an earned milestone circle in Explorer, to see the milestone board. Every badge shows its earned date. Select an achievement and choose View in Explorer to visit its place in the sequence. A badge records a past achievement; the rim around its Explorer circle shows the current recall evidence.

Milestone badges is on by default. Turn it off in Settings or the introduction to hide badge presentation while keeping test results and achievements saved. Celebrations use a softly settling badge, twinkling stars, and optional haptics. Reduce Motion uses a still badge with one quiet tap when haptics are on. Entering the next group dismisses the celebration and stops its remaining taps.

Test your memory

Choose Test my memory in Your memory or Settings. You can start before completing a lesson and continue beyond your practice place.

  • Memory only. No Guide, Watch, hints, skipping, or timer. The fourth digit checks the whole group.
  • Four attempts per group. A miss retries the same group automatically. Success advances and refills the four marks around the orb. Four misses end the test.
  • Finish whenever you like. Use Finish to end the test; Settings remains available. Explore and Your memory are unavailable during the test.
Result Meaning
Uninterrupted recall Whole groups of four before the first mistake. A correct retry does not restart or extend this score.
Digits checked Each submitted group once, including misses. Retries add no extra digits.
Personal best Compared with saved tests from the same starting place. Saved anchor tests have a separate best.

The four attempts let you continue after a mistake; they do not extend your uninterrupted score. For example, 32 digits of uninterrupted recall means the first eight groups were correct before a miss in digits 33–36. If 44 digits were checked, eleven different groups were submitted, including any incorrect groups. Retries do not add extra digits.

Tests preserve lessons and review dates. Return to practice restores your saved place, region, and input. An unfinished test also survives closing the app. Older results may retain earlier scoring rules.

iPhone, Duo, and iPad

Stillvaro is a native iPhone and iPad app for iOS/iPadOS 26 or later. iPhone supports portrait and landscape, including iPhone Duo’s outer and inner displays. Opening or closing the display keeps the current practice and entered digits. Landscape places the circular dial beside the sequence, with navigation and Practice options above the sequence when space permits. Roomy Duo and iPad landscape layouts show curved Memory, Guide, and Watch controls above the dial. Compact or short windows and larger accessibility text keep these choices in Practice options. Compact layouts keep native toolbar or side-bar navigation. On the Duo inner display, the dial and the complete sequence stay on opposite sides of the hinge: sequence above and dial below in portrait, or side by side in landscape. Explorer keeps your selected place as the layout changes. On iPad, Stillvaro uses one resizable window and follows system rotation. The dial, mode controls, navigation buttons, and sequence text grow in roomy windows and adapt down as the window gets smaller. Choose Practice, Explore π, Spiral, or Your memory in the navigation; narrow windows use a destination menu. Focus on practice reduces the history. Scroll when more room is needed. Your input and place stay saved while you resize.

Choose Settings → Layout → Dial position → Left or Right to choose the dial’s side in landscape on iPhone and iPad. Curved mode controls stay with the dial; the sequence and floating navigation toolbar use the other side. The choice is saved. Portrait and narrow windows stack the sequence above the dial. Open Memory Spiral from its toolbar button to explore your journey or more of π. In the expanded Duo landscape layout, Explorer keeps the selected shape clear on one panel while the drawing continues behind floating controls on the other. The Memory Spiral fills the canvas behind its controls. Its My journey / Explore π switch is centred over the crease, and playback speed and position stay together on the right panel.

Settings, accessibility, and saving

Settings → Extra guidance controls glyph hints and the next-digit highlight. Tests always hide hints. Settings also offers System/Light/Dark appearance and haptics.

With larger text, find Explore, Watch, and playback speed in Practice options. VoiceOver labels the controls and waits for spoken digits during Watch. The app follows text-size, contrast, transparency, and motion preferences. Explorer uses a solid control surface with Reduce Transparency; larger accessibility text can put controls on separate rows with vertical scrolling. Replay the introduction is in Settings → Help; the Continue Practice shortcut opens saved practice.

Practice, input, reviews, tests, milestone achievements and their dates, previously saved anchors, and settings stay saved locally. There is no automatic cloud sync, progress import/restore, or Home Screen widget. The app keeps a previous valid save for recovery and tells you if it restores that copy. Follow any recovery notice; a save from a newer version requires an app update.

Erase all progress and settings removes local records and preferences after confirmation. It cannot be undone. Manage device backups separately.

For help, choose Settings → Support and include the app version, iPhone or iPad model, OS version, and what happened. Practice history is not automatically sent.
